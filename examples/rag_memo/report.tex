\begin{abstract}
We compare two versions of a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline on latency and
evaluate several retrieval configurations on answer quality. Over \val{llm.latency.v1.n_traces}
and \val{llm.latency.v2.n_traces} traces, the median end-to-end latency fell from
\valu{llm.latency.v1.e2e_median_ms} in \val{llm.latency.baseline_version} to
\valu{llm.latency.v2.e2e_median_ms} in \val{llm.latency.candidate_version}, a
\valu{llm.latency.reduction_pct} reduction. On the evaluation set, the best configuration
reached a mean correctness of \val{llm.eval.rerank_hyde.correctness.mean}
(\val{llm.eval.rerank_hyde.correctness.ci_low} to \val{llm.eval.rerank_hyde.correctness.ci_high},
bootstrap CI) versus \val{llm.eval.baseline.correctness.mean} for the baseline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Latency}
The retrieval stage median dropped from \valu{llm.latency.v1.retrieve.median_ms} to
\valu{llm.latency.v2.retrieve.median_ms}, and reranking from
\valu{llm.latency.v1.rerank.median_ms} to \valu{llm.latency.v2.rerank.median_ms}. Generation was
essentially unchanged (\valu{llm.latency.v1.generate.median_ms} versus
\valu{llm.latency.v2.generate.median_ms}) and remains the dominant cost
(Figure~\ref{fig:llm.latency.stacked}, Table~\ref{tab:llm.latency.by_stage}). The p95
end-to-end latency of \valu{llm.latency.v2.e2e_p95_ms} in \val{llm.latency.candidate_version}
was lower than the \valu{llm.latency.v1.e2e_p95_ms} of the baseline.

\section{Answer quality}
Reranking improved retrieval MRR from \val{llm.eval.baseline.retrieval_mrr.mean} to
\val{llm.eval.rerank.retrieval_mrr.mean}; adding query expansion (HyDE) raised it further to
\val{llm.eval.rerank_hyde.retrieval_mrr.mean} (Table~\ref{tab:llm.eval.metrics}). Faithfulness
was similar across configurations (\val{llm.eval.baseline.faithfulness.mean},
\val{llm.eval.rerank.faithfulness.mean} and \val{llm.eval.rerank_hyde.faithfulness.mean}).
